Common Questions About Sinus Care

What does a sinus specialist treat?

A sinus specialist, usually an ENT doctor, diagnoses and treats conditions such as chronic sinusitis, recurring sinus infections, sinus pressure, nasal congestion, nasal blockage and sinus-related headaches. Balloon Sinuplasty Doctors in Queens, NY provides evaluation and care for patients dealing with these problems.

What is balloon sinuplasty?

Balloon sinuplasty, also called a balloon sinus procedure or balloon sinus dilation, is a minimally invasive treatment in which a small balloon catheter is used to widen a blocked sinus opening so the sinus can drain. Whether it is right for you depends on your symptoms, exam and imaging.

How do I know if my sinusitis is chronic?

Sinusitis is generally considered chronic when symptoms such as congestion, drainage, facial pressure and reduced smell last twelve weeks or longer, or when infections keep returning. An ENT evaluation can confirm the diagnosis and find the cause.

Is every sinus headache really a sinus problem?

No. Many headaches described as sinus headaches are actually migraines or tension headaches. If your headaches come with congestion, thick drainage and facial pressure, a sinus evaluation can help find out whether the sinuses are involved.

Chronic Sinusitis Therapy

Sinusitis is usually considered chronic when symptoms such as congestion, thick drainage, facial pressure and a reduced sense of smell last for twelve weeks or longer. Effective chronic sinusitis therapy looks for the reason the sinuses are not draining. Common causes include ongoing inflammation, allergies, nasal polyps, a deviated septum and swollen tissue around the sinus openings. Treatment often starts with medical care such as saline rinses, prescription nasal sprays, allergy management and, when appropriate, a course of medication. Procedures are considered when medical therapy has not given enough relief.

Balloon Sinus Procedure and Dilation

A balloon sinus procedure, often called balloon sinuplasty, is a minimally invasive way to open a blocked sinus passage. Instead of cutting away tissue, the doctor guides a thin, flexible catheter with a small balloon into the blocked opening, gently inflates the balloon to widen the passage and then removes it. Not everyone qualifies for balloon sinus dilation. It tends to be considered for people with chronic or frequently recurring sinusitis whose symptoms have not improved enough with medical treatment and whose scan shows a blocked sinus opening. Your doctor will tell you honestly whether this option fits your situation.

Sinus Infection Therapy

A sinus infection can cause facial pain, thick yellow or green drainage, a blocked nose, fever and pressure that worsens when you bend forward. Many mild infections improve on their own or with supportive care, but infections that last more than ten days, keep returning or come with severe symptoms should be evaluated. Targeted sinus infection therapy begins by deciding whether the problem is viral, bacterial or allergy related, because the right treatment depends on the cause. Repeated infections also raise the question of why the sinuses are not draining, which is exactly what an ENT evaluation can answer.

Practical Tips for Better Sinus Relief

Use Saline Rinses Correctly

Rinsing the nose with saline can thin mucus and wash out irritants, and many patients find it one of the most helpful daily habits. Use distilled, sterile or previously boiled and cooled water, and clean your rinse bottle after each use. Never use plain tap water straight from the faucet for nasal rinsing.

Add Moisture to the Air

Dry indoor air, especially during heating season in Queens, can irritate the nose and thicken mucus. A clean humidifier, a warm shower and plenty of fluids can ease discomfort. Keep humidifiers clean so that they do not grow mold.

Be Careful with Decongestant Sprays

Over-the-counter decongestant nasal sprays can bring quick relief, but using them for more than a few days can cause rebound congestion that makes the blockage worse. If you find yourself depending on a spray, it is time to see a doctor for a better plan.

Manage Allergies Early

Allergies swell the lining of the nose and block sinus drainage, which sets the stage for infections. Keep windows closed on high-pollen days, wash bedding regularly, shower after spending time outdoors and discuss medication options with your doctor before your difficult season begins.

Avoid Smoke and Strong Irritants

Cigarette smoke, strong fragrances and some cleaning products irritate the nose and sinuses and can keep inflammation going. Reducing exposure often helps symptoms settle.

Know When Symptoms Are an Emergency

Seek urgent medical care if a sinus infection is accompanied by a severe headache, high fever, a stiff neck, confusion, swelling or redness around the eye, changes in vision or double vision. These can be signs of a serious complication. If you have trouble breathing, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.

Understanding How Sinuses Work

The sinuses are air-filled spaces in the bones of the face. They are lined with tissue that makes mucus, and tiny openings allow that mucus to drain into the nose. When swelling, polyps or structural problems block those openings, mucus becomes trapped, pressure builds and bacteria can grow. That is why one blocked passage can cause pain, congestion and repeated infections, and why opening the passage can bring relief for the right patient.

What Recovery Can Look Like

Recovery depends on the treatment you receive and on your own health.
